# Seven Forty-Nine — 749 SF detached ADU
### 3010 S Artesia St, Santa Ana, CA 92704

Design-development set for a two-bedroom, one-bath detached accessory dwelling
unit. Not construction documents; not stamped.

The drawing set is [`drawings.html`](drawings.html) — site plan, floor plan,
four elevations, a building section, and three compliance matrices (zoning,
building, energy/green/fees).

## Issue 02 — elongated

Issue 01 was a near-square block, 28'-0" × 27'-6" (1.02:1). Issue 02 is the
same 749 SF drawn as a **38'-0" × 21'-0" bar (1.81:1)**.

On a 120-foot-deep lot that is not a preference. The square block reached
33'-6" back from the rear property line; the bar reaches 27'-0". Against a
house whose rear wall sits about 62' from the street, that is the difference
between roughly 24 feet of rear yard and **31 feet**.

Elongation also sorts the plan by orientation. Every long wall gets one job:

- **East wall** faces the yard and the house — entry loggia, 6' slider, both
  bedroom windows, 24" eave.
- **West wall** sits 6'-0" off the rear property line — kitchen run, bath,
  laundry, almost no glass, 12" eave.
- The two short **gable ends** face the side property lines.

Cost of the change: net livable area falls from 663 SF to **656 SF** (longer
perimeter, and the compact 26 SF vestibule grows to a 48 SF entry hall to
reach both bedrooms across the bar's width). Seven square feet for thirty feet
of yard.

## The two constants

**749 SF measured to the outside face of the walls.** Gov. Code §66311.5
(formerly §66324, renumbered by SB 543 effective 1 Jan 2026) bars any impact
fee on an ADU of 750 SF or less of interior livable space. Drawing to 749 SF
*gross* clears the line under the strictest reading a plan checker can apply;
on the interior measure the city uses it is 656 SF.

The number is exact: a 38'-0" × 21'-0" rectangle is 798 SF, less a 7'-0" ×
7'-0" entry loggia notched out of the southeast corner (49 SF) = **749**.

**6'-0" setbacks instead of the 4'-0" the city allows.** At 6'-0" on the rear
and north side with 12" eaves and rakes on those faces, every wall and every
roof edge holds 5'-0"+ from a property line, which puts the building outside
CRC Table R302.1(1) entirely — no one-hour assemblies, no 25% opening cap, no
protected soffits.

## Property

3010 S Artesia St is a 1,489 SF, 4-bedroom, 2-bath single-family residence
built in 1973, in the 92704 tract off S Artesia.

**The parcel is not verified.** The Orange County Assessor and City of Santa
Ana property portals were both unreachable from the session that produced this
set. 3009 S Artesia, across the street, is on a 7,218 SF lot; the street
averages about 6,300 SF. A **60' × 120' parcel is assumed**, with S Artesia on
the east. It is the single number most worth confirming first — it sets the
setbacks, the coverage, and where the bar lands. If 3010 is on the east side of
the street, mirror the site plan.

Lot coverage with the ADU: **36.7%** on 7,200 SF (44.0% on 6,000 SF), against a
50% R1 maximum.

Two Issue 01 redlines are closed by the address: a 1973 tract in 92704 sits
outside Santa Ana's historic districts, and the existing attached garage and
driveway already carry the parking requirement without new paving.

## Verify before a permit set

Pull the parcel record and get a survey. Confirm SAMC Sec. 41-194 as adopted
(the city figures here come from published summaries — santa-ana.org and
Municode were not reachable). Check the half-mile transit distance, which would
raise the height ceiling from 16' to 18'. Confirm SAFD's hose-pull measurement
(this layout runs ≈118' from the curb against a 150' limit) and that the 1973
house is unsprinklered. Have the energy consultant size the PV array on the
CF1R. Get a geotechnical report — parts of Santa Ana are in a state-mapped
liquefaction zone. Price the sewer and water runs; the bar sits about 100' back
from the street.

Structural, geotechnical, Title 24 energy, and MEP design by others.
