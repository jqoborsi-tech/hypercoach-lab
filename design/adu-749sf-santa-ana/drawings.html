The brief

One square foot is the whole argument

California prohibits any impact fee on an accessory dwelling unit of 750 square feet or less. Above that line, fees are charged proportionally against the primary dwelling — in Orange County commonly five figures. So the unit is drawn at 749 square feet, measured to the outside face of the exterior walls, which is the most conservative reading a plan checker can apply. Under the interior-livable-space measure the city actually uses, it comes in at 663 SF, with 86 SF of margin.

Everything else follows from three site facts. The lot is a standard Santa Ana R1 parcel, 50 feet wide and 135 deep, with the house at the street and a detached garage mid-lot. The rear yard is deep enough that the ADU does not need the four-foot setback concession — and that turns out to be the second design decision worth making.

Why the setbacks are six feet, not four

Santa Ana allows a new detached ADU at a 4′‑0″ rear and interior side setback. Taking it caps the building at 16 feet and, more expensively, puts two walls inside the fire-separation distance where CRC Table R302.1(1) demands one-hour rated assemblies, limits openings to 25% of wall area, and requires protected eaves.

Pushing the building to 6′‑0″ on the east side and rear puts every wall and every eave edge at 5′‑0″ or more from a property line. No rated assemblies. No opening-area limit. Ordinary eaves. The cost is two feet of rear yard the lot can easily spare; the saving is real construction money and a much easier plan check.

How it is organized

The plan is a simple bar, long side east–west, with the great room across the north face looking back at the main house and both bedrooms across the rear. Circulation is a single 4′-deep vestibule — 26 SF, 3.5% of the floor area — that touches both bedrooms and the bath. The entry is a 5′‑3″ × 4′‑0″ notch cut out of the northwest corner: it makes a covered landing without adding roof area, and because it is carved out rather than added on, it is what brings the footprint from 770 SF down to exactly 749.

Private outdoor space sits on the west, the 16′ side yard, where a 12′ × 14′ patio sits off a 6′ slider. Santa Ana is Climate Zone 8 and that west exposure is the hot one, so it is shaded by a 6′-deep trellis rather than left open.

Before submittal

Redlines

Eight things a Santa Ana plan checker will ask about that no drawing can settle on its own. I could not reach santa‑ana.org or the Municode text from this session — the zoning figures above come from published summaries of SAMC 41‑194 and from state law, so confirm the city numbers against the current adopted ordinance before you draw a permit set.

  1. Confirm SAMC 41‑194 as adopted. Specifically the detached maximum floor area, the height ceiling, and whether the city measures ADU area as gross or interior livable space. The design is drawn to survive the stricter reading of each, but the numbers in the matrix should be verified line by line.
  2. Parking waiver. Measure the ½-mile walking distance to the nearest transit stop. If it qualifies, the 9′×18′ pad becomes optional and can be returned to yard.
  3. Historic district. Santa Ana has several — French Park, Floral Park, Washington Square, Wilshire Square, Heninger Park, Lacy, Logan. Inside one, materials and placement pick up an added review and the stucco/board-and-batten palette may need to change.
  4. Fire access. The hose pull measures roughly 129′ from the curb. Confirm how SAFD measures it on this parcel; if it lands over 150′, expect a request for residential sprinklers or a widened path.
  5. Sprinklers. State law protects the ADU only if the primary dwelling is also unsprinklered. Confirm the existing house has no system and that no sprinkler retrofit is being triggered.
  6. Solar. Have the energy consultant run the array size on the CF1R. If it comes back at or above the exemption threshold, the west slope carries the panels — the solar-ready zone is sized for it and the roof orientation already suits it.
  7. Geotechnical. Parts of Santa Ana sit in a state-mapped liquefaction zone. A soils report may be required, and it drives the foundation design; do not assume a conventional slab.
  8. Utilities. Confirm whether the city requires a separate sewer lateral and water service for a detached ADU on this parcel, and get the connection-fee calculation in writing — it must be proportionate to size.

Assumptions this set is drawn on

A flat interior R1 lot, 50′×135′, not in a historic district, flood zone or very-high fire hazard severity zone; an existing 1,148 SF single-family house at a 20′ front setback with a detached 400 SF garage mid-lot; an existing unsprinklered primary dwelling; and utilities with capacity at the street. Change any of those and the site plan moves before the floor plan does. The building itself — 28′‑0″ × 27′‑6″ with the entry notched out of one corner — is portable to any Santa Ana R1 rear yard that can give it 34′ of depth and 34′ of width.
