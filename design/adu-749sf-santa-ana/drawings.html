Issue 03 — what the survey changed

The lot is half as deep as assumed, so the bar gets longer and thinner

The topographic survey puts the rear property line 33′‑4″ behind the house, not the 60-odd feet the neighbourhood averages suggested. That kills the 38′ × 21′ bar of Issue 02 outright — it would have left six feet between buildings. The same 749 SF is redrawn as 17′‑6″ × 44′‑0″, a 2.51:1 bar laid along the rear fence, which is the only shape that fits a 56′ × 33′ yard with a real gap left in front of it.

The depth is set by arithmetic, not taste. Five feet off the rear line keeps the west wall unrated. Ten feet ten inches to the house lets an assumed property line sit between the two buildings with more than five feet to each wall, which is what CRC R302.1 needs to leave both walls unrated. 33′‑4″ − 5′‑0″ − 10′‑10″ = 17′‑6″. The length then follows: 56′ less 6′‑0″ each side is 44′.

A bar this long cannot use the side-by-side bedroom plan of Issues 01 and 02 — at 16′‑4″ inside, two bedrooms across the width would be eight feet wide. So the plan changes shape: a bedroom at each end, the great room in the middle, and the entry cut into the east face at mid-length. Bedroom 1 sits behind a four-foot hall and the bath; Bedroom 2 opens off the far end of the great room with its closet on the shared wall as the sound buffer. Both bedrooms are 16′‑4″ wide and 8′‑6″ deep, with a window to the courtyard and their escape casement in the gable end facing the side yard rather than the neighbour’s house ten feet away.

Net livable area is 651 SF — five less than Issue 02, twelve less than the square block — and circulation is back down to 24 SF, because a mid-bar entry means nobody walks the length of the building to reach a door.

What stays and what goes

The existing patio off the house’s southwest corner survives; the bar sits west of it and the patio becomes the south end of a 10′‑10″ courtyard between the two buildings. The storage shed at the southwest corner, the concrete walk to it, and two trees — a 17-inch specimen and a 12-inch — are inside the footprint and come out. The unpaved path along the house’s west wall becomes the paved path of travel to the entry, fed from the gate on the north side.

749 is still exact

17′‑6″ × 44′‑0″ is 770 SF. The recessed entry, 4′‑0″ deep × 5′‑3″ wide, removes 21. 770 − 21 = 749, to the outside face of the walls. On the interior measure the city uses it is 651 SF, with 99 SF of margin under Gov. Code §66311.5.

Before submittal

Redlines

The survey closed the biggest question from Issue 02. What it opened is more specific: a tight lot, two buildings ten feet apart, and one narrow way in.

  1. Lot coverage. 44.5% with the house footprint scaled from the survey, 48.8% if the existing patio is a roofed structure. State ADU law protects a unit of 800 SF or less from denial on coverage grounds, so the ADU is not at risk — but get the city’s current R1 cap and its treatment of the patio in writing, because it constrains anything the owners want to add later.
  2. The house’s own eave. The assumed property line sits 5′‑5″ from each wall. The ADU’s east eave takes a Type X soffit as drawn; the house’s existing west eave will also fall inside 5′ of that line. Confirm with Building & Safety whether they want it protected, the line shifted, or nothing — practice varies.
  3. Trees. The 17-inch and 12-inch trees are inside the footprint and come out. Six 8–12 inch trees along the west block wall stand about three feet from the new footing; expect to lose most of them. None are street trees, so no permit, but check the tree ordinance.
  4. The north side passage. 4′‑10″ clear, gated, and it is the path of travel, the fire hose route, the utility trench and the only way to get materials in. Verify nothing on the house’s north wall (meters, vents, hose bibs) narrows it below 3′‑6″.
  5. Drainage. The yard falls toward the house. The design puts the pad on the high side and drains the courtyard north; confirm the side passage can carry it to the driveway and that the house’s FF (38.08) stays above the swale.
  6. The usual four. Confirm SAMC 41‑194 as adopted; the ½-mile transit distance (raises the height cap to 18′); that the 1973 house is unsprinklered; the CF1R solar array; and a geotechnical report — the benchmark is NAVD88 33.2 at MacArthur, this is flat alluvial ground in a mapped liquefaction area.

What is assumed now

Much less than before. The lot, the house position, the walls, the trees and the grades are from the Foresight survey. Still assumed: the house’s footprint east of the pathway (scaled, not measured), that the patio is uncovered, that the north gate is at least 3′ wide, and that no easement crosses the rear five feet. The building — 17′‑6″ × 44′‑0″ with the entry recessed mid-length — is now specific to this yard.
